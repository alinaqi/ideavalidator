## Your tasks for this stage
1. **Classify the category.** Choose the single best `primary_category` key from the taxonomy above and
   any `secondary_categories`. Explain the choice. Be precise about the JOB: e.g. a tool that gives an
   EXISTING agent a new ability (ai_action_layer) is a different category from a standalone app the user
   talks to (consumer_app) — do not conflate them.
2. **Name the real incumbents/substitutes FOR THAT CATEGORY only.** List who a buyer would actually
   compare this to. Explicitly exclude look-alikes from other categories and say why they are NOT
   substitutes (this prevents the classic error of comparing a connector to a standalone app).
3. **Enumerate candidate business models** for this idea, drawn from the category's common models AND the
   archetype list — include at least one **business-model-innovation** option (a way to win by changing
   the model, pricing or packaging, not just the product). Recommend one, with the trade-off.
4. **Fill the Business Model Canvas** (all nine blocks): customer_segments, value_propositions, channels,
   customer_relationships, revenue_streams, key_resources, key_activities, key_partnerships,
   cost_structure. One crisp line per block; mark each HYPOTHESIS or EVIDENCED.
5. **Define the USP and positioning.** Write a one-sentence `usp` (what is uniquely true and defensible
   vs the category incumbents — the wedge, not a feature list) and a `positioning_statement`
   ("For [actor] who [need], [product] is a [category] that [unique benefit], unlike [incumbent].").
6. **Value positioning map.** Pick the two axes that matter most to buyers in this category
   (`value_positioning.axes.x_label`, `.y_label`), place this idea (`our_position`, x/y in 0-100) and the
   key incumbents (`competitors`, each x/y in 0-100) so the whitespace is visible.

7. **Do not over-qualify.** Classify the idea AS STATED. If a *different* category or business model
   would be bigger or more defensible (e.g. selling infra B2B instead of the stated consumer tool), put
   it in `adjacent_opportunities` with why it is bigger and why it is a SEPARATE business — do not make
   it the recommendation.

## Scoring criteria (0-5)
category_confidence (how clearly this maps to one category and the right incumbents),
incumbent_fit (are the listed incumbents the true substitutes, not look-alikes),
model_fit (does at least one viable business model — possibly innovative — fit the category and founder),
usp_distinctiveness (is the USP a real, defensible wedge vs those incumbents, not a feature).

## Kill flags for this stage
`no_defensible_category` (fits no category and has no novel one), `incumbent_owns_the_wedge`
(the single obvious incumbent already does exactly this with no gap).
