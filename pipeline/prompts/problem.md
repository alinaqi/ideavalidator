## Your tasks for this stage
Validate that the **problem** exists independently of our solution, using desk evidence only
(PDLC Customer Research: story/behaviour over hypothetical preference; PD-C013).
1. **Problem signals in the wild**: forum threads, reviews of incumbent tools complaining about this
   exact job, support communities, LinkedIn/Twitter posts, conference talks. Quote the language people
   actually use (verbatim, short, with URL) — this feeds positioning later.
2. **Demand proxies**: search terms people use (and any volume data you can find), communities and
   their size, job postings that pay humans to do this job today, consultants/agencies charging for it,
   spreadsheets/templates/no-code hacks people built. Each is a behavioural signal.
3. **Existing spend**: what do people already pay (money or hours) to solve or tolerate this problem?
   Estimate the **status quo cost** per actor per month with sourced inputs.
4. **Hair-on-fire test** (Sequoia): is this a problem people are actively trying to solve now, a hard
   fact they tolerate, or a future-vision problem? Justify.
5. **Who feels it most**: which sub-segments show the strongest/most frequent signals? (This seeds ICP.)
6. **Contrary evidence**: who says this is not a problem, or that existing solutions are fine?
7. Draft a **Mom Test interview guide** (Rob Fitzpatrick): 8-10 questions about past behaviour, no
   pitching, no hypotheticals, for the founder to run with 5-10 real people next week.

## Scoring criteria
problem_evidence (independent public evidence that the problem exists), frequency (how often the
actor hits it), severity (consequence when it happens), existing_spend (money/time already spent on
workarounds — the strongest behavioural signal), urgency (hair-on-fire vs nice-to-have).

## Kill flags for this stage
